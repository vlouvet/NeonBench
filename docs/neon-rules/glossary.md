# Glossary of neon trade terms

Compiled from the four PDFs. Citations in the form `(SN p.X)` for *Saving Neon*, `(B1)`/`(B2)`/`(B3)` for Blazek Books 1/2/3.

## Construction & geometry

- **Single tube / single stroke** *(B1, B2, B3)*. A letter built from one tube outlining the stroke as a thin line. Each Blazek letter has a "SINGLE TUBE" page.
- **Double tube / double stroke / powdered unit** *(B1)*. A letter built from two parallel tubes following the inside and outside of a thick stroke. "Powdered" = phosphor-coated, common companion technique.
- **Bend (numbered)** *(B1, B2, B3)*. Each letter pattern has a sequence of small numbered circles showing the order in which the bender works each bend.
- **DB / Double Back / 180° Bend** *(B1)*. A 180° hairpin returning the tube to roughly the same direction it came from. **Standard construction**, not an error condition. Abbreviated "DB" on Blazek's layouts.
- **Rise / Drop** *(B1)*. A Z-shaped offset that lifts (or lowers) the tube to a different parallel level. Example: bends 6 and 8 of letter "E".
- **Rise Turn / Drop Turn** *(B1)*. A combined offset+turn — the tube changes both its parallel level and its direction.
- **90° Bend** *(B1)*. A right-angle corner. Example: first bend of letter "E".
- **50° Bend** *(B1)*. An acute bend. Example: bends 1 and 2 of letter "Z". (The "50°" is illustrative; trade reality is "any acute angle".)
- **Crossfire Flyover with Rise and Drop** *(B1)*. A script bend produced "in crossfire" — the burner crosses the tube at an angle — combined with a rise and drop. Used in script alphabets, e.g. single-stroke "X", script "B".
- **Ribbon Fire Flyover** *(B1)*. A bend "produced in one ribbon heat", primarily in script alphabets. Examples: script "D", script "H".
- **Double Back with Angle** *(B1)*. A double-back combined with a directional change. Example: first bend of "V", "W".
- **Ribbon Heat** *(B2)*. A single broad heated section on the ribbon burner used for long, soft script curves.
- **Correction Bend** *(B2)*. A small remedial bend used to bring the in-progress tube back onto the pattern.
- **Reheat & Close** *(B1 letter pages, e.g. "B")*. Reheating a previously-made bend so the tube can be closed (welded shut after a tube-end manipulation).
- **Close** *(B1, B2 letter pages)*. The final step of joining a bent tube to itself or to another piece. Often paired with a weld.
- **Yield** *(B1 letter pages)*. A small relaxation/give-back motion in the bend, used to relieve stress in tight forms.
- **Weld / splice** *(SN p.19; B1 letter "H")*. Glass-to-glass join made by reheating two tube ends and fusing them. Used when a letter is split into halves.

## Pumping & gas filling

- **Bombardment** *(SN p.19)*. The process of removing impurities from the inside of a sealed tube before the noble gas is introduced. High current is run through the tube to ionize and pump out residual gas.
- **Vacuum-sealed** *(SN p.19)*. After bombardment, the tube is evacuated, the noble gas is admitted at low pressure, and the tube is sealed.
- **Pump** *(B1 intro)*. Verb. The full evacuate+fill+seal operation. "Easier to bend, pump, install and service this way."
- **Drop of mercury** *(SN p.20)*. A small quantity of liquid mercury introduced into argon-filled tubes; vapor pressure provides the UV that activates the phosphor coating.

## Electrodes & wiring

- **Electrode** *(SN p.19)*. A metal cathode/anode welded onto each end of a sealed tube. Carries current from the GTO wire into the gas.
- **Brass electrode button** *(SN p.21)*. A brass cap on the end of the electrode; improves connection and resists corrosion.
- **Electrode center / housing center** *(B1 intro, ♦ symbol)*. The point on the pattern where the electrode and its housing meet the substrate. Diamond ♦ marks this on Blazek pages.
- **Housing / glass housing / porcelain housing** *(SN pp.22-23)*. The insulating cup/sleeve, fitted into a hole in the sign face, that supports the electrode end of a tube. Contains a spring contact. Glass or porcelain. Never to be replaced with a smaller-diameter "bushing".
- **Tube support / spacer / standoff** *(SN p.6, p.23)*. A small glass insulator wired to the substrate, holding the body of the tube in its z-offset position away from the painted face.
- **GTO wire / GTO cable** *(SN p.21)*. "Gas Tube Only" — high-voltage ignition wire connecting transformer to electrode. Spaced ≥3 in (76 mm) apart, kept as short as possible, never crossing.
- **Rubber boot / silicone end cap** *(SN p.22)*. A flexible cover used to weatherproof an electrode-to-wire join — explicitly **forbidden** as a shortcut for re-engineering double-back electrodes (banned for vintage-sign restoration). Code allowed them only after the mid-1990s.
- **Double-backed electrode** *(SN p.3, p.22)*. An electrode geometry where the electrode lead doubles back to exit the same side of the letter as the other electrode. Used for **window signs**. Should be designed in, not retrofitted with rubber boots.
- **Series wiring** *(SN p.22)*. Two or more tubes wired in series on a single transformer.

## Substrate / cabinet / paint

- **Cabinet** *(SN throughout)*. The sheet-metal housing of a sign. Contains transformers, sequencers, internal wiring.
- **Access panel** *(SN p.24)*. The little doors on the sign cabinet for service access.
- **Block-out paint** *(SN p.19)*. Black paint applied to (a) the area between letters where the tube jumps between strokes, and (b) the section of glass tube that passes through the housing. Hides the un-illuminated portions so only the intended letter glows.
- **Pattern / paper pattern** *(SN p.18)*. The 1:1 paper drawing the bender works against. Marks letterforms, electrode/housing centers ("circled x"), and bend types. **The accuracy of the pattern is the foundation of the build** — "If the pattern is off even a quarter of an inch, the glass might not fit."
- **Center-to-center measurement** *(SN p.18)*. Distance between two housing-hole centers on the substrate; the dimension the tube ends must hit precisely.
- **Channel letters** *(SN p.37)*. Sheet-metal U-channel forming a 3D letter shape; tubing runs inside the channel.

## Animation & control

- **Sequencer / sequencing unit** *(SN p.25, p.36)*. A mechanical or electronic switching unit that turns parts of a sign on and off in sequence to create animation.
- **Service timer** *(SN pp.3, 21)*. A timer on the supply circuit so the sign turns off overnight. Energy conservation.
- **Service switch** *(SN p.21)*. A disconnect on the sign for service work.

## Transformers & power

- **Transformer** *(SN throughout)*. Steps line voltage up to several kV to drive the gas discharge. Two types in current use: **electromagnetic** (preferred for outdoor, longer life) and **solid-state** (cheaper, shorter outdoor life).
- **Pull-chain transformer** *(SN p.3)*. A transformer with an integrated mechanical pull-switch. Forbidden for restoration — failure-prone.
- **4 watts per foot** *(SN p.36)*. Rule of thumb for power consumption of a neon tube.

## Color & coating

- **Phosphor-coated** *(SN p.20)*. Inside surface of glass tube coated with phosphor. Looks white in daylight, glows in the phosphor's color when energized. Typically fills with argon + Hg.
- **Powdered (tube/unit)** *(B1 intro)*. Trade synonym for phosphor-coated.
- **Novial** *(SN p.20)*. Trade name for an imported tinted glass tube (e.g. Novial Gold).
- **Mercury stain** *(SN p.20)*. Dark spot on an old tube's glass where mercury has discolored the inside. Tube needs replacement.

## Roles

- **Tube bender** *(SN p.19)*. The skilled craftsperson who heats and bends the glass. "Twenty-plus years to perfect this skill."
- **Sign shop / sign contractor** *(SN throughout)*. The business that handles the full restoration or build.
